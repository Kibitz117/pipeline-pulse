Why these operator changes surfaced

The screen filters for operational changes large or urgent enough to merit analyst attention. This is not an estimate of price impact or affected U.S. supply.

  • Outage forecast: a revision must change capacity by at least 25,000 Dth/day. Points then reflect absolute size, percentage change, and how soon it takes effect.
  • Operating capacity: an update surfaces when capacity moves at least 50,000 Dth/day, scheduled utilization crosses 80% or 95%, available capacity moves to or from zero, or availability moves at least 100,000 Dth/day while utilization is 80% or higher.
  • New notice: points reflect subject matter—maintenance, constraint, emergency, curtailment, or OFO—and time until effective.
  • Same-ID notice revision: every change to parsed status, prior-notice link, subject, text, effective window, or response requirement surfaces. Its detection time—not the operator’s original post time—controls point-in-time replay.

Gas market guide

From a maintenance notice to market impact

A reduction changes transportation capacity first. It matters for price only when the market cannot comfortably reroute, reschedule, or replace the affected gas.

Open guide
1
Operator reduces capacity

The notice identifies where, when, and by how much TGP expects transportation capacity to fall. It is a forecast operating limit—not lost production or measured flow.

2
Transport demand tests the limit

Compare the limit with nominations moving in the same direction. If scheduled transport remains above forecast capacity, shippers may need to reschedule, reroute, or reduce deliveries.

3
The region rebalances

Alternate pipelines, storage withdrawals, local production, or lower demand can absorb the constraint. If they cannot, gas may become relatively cheaper upstream and scarcer downstream.

4
Prices provide confirmation

The nearest regional basis markets should react before Henry Hub. A national benchmark effect requires the constraint to materially change the broader U.S. supply-and-demand balance.

How to use this screen: treat a conditional shortfall as a place to investigate, then look for same-cycle nominations, physical-flow or rerouting evidence, regional demand, and basis-price confirmation.

What TGP can illuminate

Regional deliverability

TGP links producing areas in Louisiana and South Texas and Northeast/Appalachian supply with demand markets including New York, Boston, the Gulf Coast, and Mexico. A constraint can change where gas is deliverable even when national production is unchanged.

  • Appalachia: constrained egress can weaken upstream producer-area prices.
  • New York and New England: constrained deliveries can tighten downstream citygate or regional basis.
  • Southbound and Gulf routes: direction determines whether the effect is on receipts, Gulf demand, exports, or alternate routing.

What remains outside the screen

The rest of the gas balance

This monitor does not observe all U.S. supply, demand, storage, LNG, production, or interstate transportation. It also does not yet include measured physical flow or a licensed regional cash-price feed.

  • Other Appalachia and Northeast corridors such as Transco, Texas Eastern, Columbia, Algonquin, and Iroquois.
  • Weather-driven demand, power burn, LNG feedgas, production revisions, and regional storage operations.
  • Regional cash basis and the current Henry Hub futures curve.
How coverage should expand

Pipeline capacities overlap because the same gas can move through several connected systems. Adding nameplate capacities would double-count molecules, so five operators cannot honestly be labeled “five times the market.” Expansion should instead report which producing basins, demand regions, and constrained corridors have maintenance, scheduling, weather, and price confirmation.

Planned reduction

How much nominal transportation capacity the outage forecast removes for a station, direction, and period.

Forecast operating capacity

How much capacity TGP expects to provide during the maintenance window.

Scheduled quantity

Net gas nominated and confirmed for transportation in that direction at the captured cycle. It is not a physical-flow meter.

Available capacity

Operating capacity remaining after scheduled quantity. Low availability indicates limited spare room at that posting—not necessarily a curtailment.

Conditional shortfall

The amount by which the captured schedule would exceed forecast capacity if it stayed unchanged through the future event. It is a scenario, not a forecast of lost gas.

A binding constraint can separate prices on either side of the bottleneck. Direction and alternate routes determine which location tightens or weakens.

Balance contextStorage and demand

High inventories or mild demand can cushion a transport problem. Tight inventories, extreme weather, or high power burn can make the same outage more consequential.

Higher barHenry Hub futures

TGP maintenance alone does not establish an NG futures view. The event must be large enough—and difficult enough to reroute—to alter expected U.S. injections, withdrawals, production, or consumption.

Evidence that strengthens the case

Updated nominations still exceed the limit; alternate routes are tight; regional demand is high; basis or flow data moves in the expected direction.

Evidence that weakens the case

TGP revises the work away; schedules fall below the limit; shippers reroute successfully; regional prices and flows show no stress.
